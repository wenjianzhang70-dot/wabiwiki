Wabi AI Review: Our First Look

Basis: official announcements, the founder's launch walkthrough and reputable launch coverage (October 2026). Wabi is days old — this is a first look, not a long-term test, and we'll revise as the product settles.

What's genuinely new

  • Multiplayer is real, not a gimmick. Shared threads where an assistant plans with your partner or family addresses something ChatGPT-style apps structurally can't do.
  • Mini-apps beat text walls. A poll, tracker or itinerary you can tap is simply better than a paragraph describing one.
  • Threads fix the “one endless chat” problem. Context stays where it belongs.

The trade-offs

  • Trust is the product's price of entry. The best features need email, calendar — even banking — access. The per-chat scoping helps, but you're trusting a young startup. See our safety breakdown.
  • Business model: unknown. Free today; the company says pricing isn't decided. Features may move behind a paywall later.
  • Brand-new and moving fast. Wabi already pivoted once (from an app-creator tool to this assistant), and the iOS build went 3.0.0 → 3.0.5 in the three weeks around launch. Expect rough edges and changes — but also quick fixes.
  • No Windows version at launch, and app-store name confusion with unrelated “Wabi” apps is real — use our download guide.

Who it's for

  • Couples, families and friend groups who coordinate a lot in chats.
  • Solo users drowning in small recurring admin (briefs, trackers, lists).
  • Replika fans curious what its founder built next.

Who should wait

  • Anyone needing a mature, stable tool for critical work today.
  • Windows-only users.
  • Anyone not comfortable connecting personal accounts to a new service.

Verdict

Wabi is one of the few AI launches with a truly different idea — an assistant for groups, not just individuals — launched by a founder with a decade-long track record in consumer AI. It's free, it's early, and the privacy trade-off is yours to judge. Worth trying; connect accounts gradually.
